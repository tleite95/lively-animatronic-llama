# Glossary of Domain-Specific Terms

## Toxicology

### Acceptable Daily Intake (ADI)
An estimate of the amount of a substance that can be ingested daily over a lifetime without appreciable health risk, usually expressed in mg/kg body weight/day.

### Adverse Outcome Pathway (AOP)
A structured representation of the sequence of biological events linking a molecular initiating event to an adverse outcome at the organism or population level.

### Benchmark Dose / Benchmark Concentration (BMD / BMC)
A modeled dose or concentration associated with a predefined change in response compared with background levels. BMD is generally used for administered dose, while BMC is used for exposure concentration.

### Benchmark Dose Lower Confidence Limit / Benchmark Concentration Lower Confidence Limit (BMDL / BMCL)
The lower confidence bound on a benchmark dose or benchmark concentration. It is often used as a conservative point of departure in risk assessment.

### Bioactivation
The metabolic conversion of a substance into a more biologically reactive or toxic form.

### Bioassay
An experimental test that uses living organisms, cells, tissues, or biological molecules to measure the potency, toxicity, or biological activity of a substance.

### Carcinogenicity
The ability of a substance, exposure, or agent to cause or promote cancer.

### Critical Effect
The adverse effect identified as occurring at the lowest relevant dose or exposure level and used as the basis for deriving a health-protective value.

### Cytotoxicity
The capacity of a substance or condition to damage or kill cells.

### Developmental Toxicity
Adverse effects on the developing organism that may result from exposure before conception, during prenatal development, or after birth.

### Dose-Response Assessment
The analysis of the relationship between the amount of exposure to a substance and the magnitude or probability of a biological effect.

### Effective Dose / Effective Concentration 50 (ED50 / EC50)
The dose or concentration that produces a specified effect in 50% of the test population or produces 50% of the maximal response, depending on the assay design.

### Genotoxicity
The ability of a substance or exposure to damage genetic material, including DNA sequence integrity, chromosomal structure, or chromosome number.

### Hepatotoxicity
Toxicity directed primarily at the liver, including structural injury, impaired function, altered enzyme activity, or cell death.

### Immunotoxicity
Adverse effects on immune system structure or function, including immune suppression, immune stimulation, hypersensitivity, or autoimmunity.

### Lethal Dose / Lethal Concentration 50 (LD50 / LC50)
The dose or concentration estimated to cause death in 50% of a test population under specified experimental conditions.

### Lowest-Observed-Adverse-Effect Level (LOAEL)
The lowest tested dose or exposure level at which a statistically or biologically significant adverse effect is observed compared with a control group.

### Lowest-Observed-Effect Level (LOEL)
The lowest tested dose or exposure level at which any treatment-related effect is observed, whether or not the effect is considered adverse.

### Maximum Tolerated Dose (MTD)
The highest dose that can be administered in a study without causing unacceptable toxicity, excessive mortality, or major impairment of normal physiology.

### Mechanism of Toxicity
The detailed biochemical, molecular, cellular, and physiological processes by which a toxicant produces an adverse effect.

### Molecular Initiating Event (MIE)
The initial interaction between a stressor and a biomolecule that begins an adverse outcome pathway.

### Mutagenicity
The ability of a substance or exposure to induce heritable changes in genetic material.

### Nephrotoxicity
Toxicity directed primarily at the kidney, including impaired filtration, tubular injury, altered excretion, or renal cell damage.

### Neurotoxicity
Adverse effects on the structure or function of the nervous system, including changes in behavior, cognition, sensation, motor control, or neural integrity.

### No-Observed-Adverse-Effect Level (NOAEL)
The highest tested dose or exposure level at which no statistically or biologically significant adverse effect is observed compared with a control group.

### No-Observed-Effect Level (NOEL)
The highest tested dose or exposure level at which no treatment-related effect of any kind is observed.

### Point of Departure (POD)
A dose or concentration selected from experimental or observational data as the starting point for deriving health-based guidance values.

### Reference Dose / Reference Concentration (RfD / RfC)
An estimate of continuous human exposure likely to be without appreciable risk of adverse effects over a specified duration. RfD is generally expressed as an oral dose, while RfC is generally expressed as an inhalation concentration.

### Reproductive Toxicity
Adverse effects on reproductive function, fertility, pregnancy outcomes, or reproductive organs in males or females.

### Safety Factor / Uncertainty Factor
A factor applied to a point of departure to account for uncertainty or variability, such as interspecies extrapolation, human variability, data gaps, or exposure duration differences.

### Systemic Toxicity
Toxicity affecting organs, tissues, or physiological systems beyond the initial site of contact.

### Target Organ Toxicity
Adverse effects occurring primarily in a specific organ or tissue as a result of exposure to a toxicant.

### Teratogenicity
The ability of a substance or exposure to cause structural or functional abnormalities during embryonic or fetal development.

### Toxicodynamics
The study of how a toxicant interacts with biological targets and produces toxic effects over time.

### Toxicokinetics
The study of the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of toxic substances in an organism.

### Toxicophore
A structural feature or chemical substructure associated with a toxicological effect or mechanism.

### ToxCast
A high-throughput screening program developed to evaluate chemicals across many biological assays and prioritize substances for further toxicological assessment.

### Tox21
A collaborative high-throughput toxicology program designed to evaluate chemicals for activity across biological pathways relevant to toxicity.


## Biomedical Sciences

### Apoptosis
A regulated form of programmed cell death characterized by controlled cellular dismantling without provoking strong inflammatory responses.

### Autophagy
A cellular degradation and recycling process in which cytoplasmic components are delivered to lysosomes for breakdown.

### Bioavailability
The fraction of an administered substance that reaches systemic circulation or a biological target in an active form.

### Biomarker
A measurable biological characteristic used as an indicator of exposure, effect, disease state, susceptibility, or response to intervention.

### Caspase
A protease involved in programmed cell death and inflammatory signaling pathways.

### Chemokine
A signaling protein that directs the movement, recruitment, or positioning of immune cells.

### Cytokine
A small signaling protein released by cells to regulate immune, inflammatory, hematopoietic, or intercellular communication processes.

### Cytochrome P450 / CYP450
A family of enzymes involved in the metabolism of endogenous molecules and xenobiotics, especially oxidative drug and chemical metabolism.

### Differentiation
The process by which a cell acquires specialized structural and functional characteristics.

### Epigenetic Modification
A heritable or persistent change in gene regulation that does not alter the underlying DNA sequence, such as DNA methylation or histone modification.

### Ex Vivo
Refers to experiments performed on cells, tissues, or organs removed from an organism but maintained under controlled conditions outside the body.

### Gene Knockdown
A reduction in expression of a specific gene, often achieved using RNA interference or related molecular techniques.

### Gene Knockout
The inactivation or removal of a specific gene to study its biological function.

### Genotype
The genetic composition of an organism, cell, or locus, often considered in relation to phenotype or disease susceptibility.

### Homeostasis
The maintenance of internal biological stability through coordinated regulatory processes.

### In Silico
Refers to computational modeling, simulation, or analysis performed using computer-based methods rather than physical experiments.

### In Situ
Refers to analysis or observation performed in the original biological location or structural context.

### In Vitro
Refers to experiments conducted outside a living organism using isolated cells, tissues, enzymes, or molecular systems.

### In Vivo
Refers to experiments or processes occurring within a living organism.

### Metabolite
A small molecule produced, transformed, or consumed during metabolism.

### Necrosis
A form of cell death associated with loss of membrane integrity and often accompanied by inflammation or tissue damage.

### Organoid
A three-dimensional cell culture system that self-organizes to mimic selected structural and functional features of an organ.

### Phenotype
The observable characteristics of a cell, organism, or biological system resulting from genetic, environmental, and developmental influences.

### Pharmacodynamics
The study of the biochemical and physiological effects of a drug or bioactive compound and its mechanism of action.

### Pharmacokinetics
The study of the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of a drug or bioactive compound.

### Proteomics
The large-scale study of proteins, including their abundance, modification, interactions, and functions.

### Transcriptomics
The large-scale study of RNA transcripts expressed by a cell, tissue, or organism under specified conditions.

### Xenobiotic
A chemical substance foreign to a biological organism, including drugs, pollutants, food additives, and industrial chemicals.


## Environmental Chemistry

### Abiotic Degradation
Chemical or physical transformation of a substance in the environment without direct biological mediation.

### Adsorption Coefficient / Distribution Coefficient (Kd)
A parameter describing the partitioning of a substance between a solid phase, such as soil or sediment, and an aqueous phase.

### Atmospheric Deposition
The transfer of gases, particles, or dissolved substances from the atmosphere to land or water surfaces through wet or dry processes.

### Bioaccumulation
The net accumulation of a substance in an organism from all exposure routes, including food, water, sediment, and air.

### Bioconcentration
The accumulation of a substance in an organism from the surrounding medium, usually water, excluding dietary uptake.

### Bioconcentration Factor (BCF)
The ratio of a chemical concentration in an organism to its concentration in the surrounding medium at steady state.

### Biodegradation
The transformation or breakdown of a substance by microorganisms or other biological processes.

### Biomagnification
The increase in chemical concentration in organisms at higher trophic levels due to dietary transfer through a food web.

### Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD)
A measure of the amount of oxygen required to chemically oxidize organic and inorganic substances in water.

### Dissolved Organic Carbon (DOC)
The fraction of organic carbon in water that passes through a specified filter, commonly used to characterize organic matter influencing contaminant transport and reactivity.

### Environmental Half-Life
The time required for the amount or concentration of a substance in an environmental compartment to decrease by half through degradation, transformation, or removal.

### Fugacity
A thermodynamic measure related to the escaping tendency of a chemical from a phase, often used to model chemical partitioning among environmental media.

### Henry’s Law Constant
A partitioning parameter describing the tendency of a chemical to distribute between air and water.

### Hydrolysis
A chemical transformation in which a substance reacts with water, often resulting in bond cleavage and formation of degradation products.

### Octanol-Air Partition Coefficient (Koa)
A parameter describing the partitioning of a chemical between octanol and air, often used to assess behavior of semi-volatile organic compounds.

### Octanol-Water Partition Coefficient (Kow)
A parameter describing the partitioning of a chemical between octanol and water, often used as an indicator of hydrophobicity and bioaccumulation potential.

### Persistent Organic Pollutant (POP)
An organic chemical that resists environmental degradation, can undergo long-range transport, bioaccumulates, and may cause adverse effects.

### Photolysis
The transformation or degradation of a chemical through absorption of light.

### Sorption
The association of a chemical with a solid or surface through adsorption, absorption, or both.

### Total Organic Carbon (TOC)
A measure of the total amount of organic carbon present in a water, soil, sediment, or waste sample.

### Vapor Pressure
A measure of a chemical’s tendency to enter the gas phase from a pure liquid or solid state.

### Volatilization
The transfer of a chemical from a liquid, solid, or surface into the gas phase.


## Exposure Science

### Absorbed Dose
The amount of a substance that crosses an absorption barrier and enters the body, tissue, or systemic circulation.

### Aggregate Exposure
Combined exposure to a single substance across multiple routes, sources, pathways, or settings.

### Applied Dose
The amount of a substance contacting an external body boundary, such as skin, lung surface, or gastrointestinal tract.

### Biomonitoring
The measurement of chemicals, metabolites, or biological response markers in human or ecological samples to assess exposure or internal dose.

### Breathing-Zone Concentration
The concentration of an airborne substance in the air near a person’s nose and mouth, used to estimate inhalation exposure.

### Cumulative Exposure
Combined exposure to multiple substances or stressors that may contribute to a common health outcome or toxicological mechanism.

### Dermal Absorption
The movement of a substance across the skin and into underlying tissue or systemic circulation.

### Exposure Factor
A parameter used to estimate exposure, such as body weight, inhalation rate, ingestion rate, contact rate, exposure frequency, or exposure duration.

### Exposure Pathway
The route by which a contaminant moves from a source through environmental media to a point of contact with a receptor.

### Exposure Route
The way a substance enters or contacts the body, such as ingestion, inhalation, dermal contact, or injection.

### External Dose
The amount of a substance available at a body boundary before absorption occurs.

### Intake Dose
The amount of a substance taken into the body through inhalation, ingestion, or other exposure routes over a specified period.

### Internal Dose
The amount of a substance that has entered the body and is available in systemic circulation, tissues, or target sites.

### Microenvironmental Exposure
Exposure occurring within a defined location or setting with distinct environmental conditions, such as a home, workplace, vehicle, or outdoor area.

### Personal Exposure Monitoring
Measurement of exposure using sampling devices or sensors worn by or located near an individual.

### Potential Dose
The amount of a substance that could be taken into the body if contact and intake occur as assumed in an exposure scenario.

### Time-Activity Pattern
A record or model of how individuals spend time across locations and activities relevant to exposure estimation.


## Epidemiology and Public Health

### Attack Rate
The proportion of a population that develops disease during a specified outbreak or exposure period.

### Attributable Risk
The difference in disease occurrence between exposed and unexposed groups, representing the excess risk associated with an exposure.

### Case-Control Study
An observational study design that compares individuals with an outcome to individuals without the outcome to evaluate prior exposures or risk factors.

### Case Definition
A set of standardized criteria used to determine whether an individual should be classified as having a disease, condition, exposure, or outcome of interest.

### Cohort Study
An observational study design in which a defined population is followed over time to compare outcome occurrence by exposure status.

### Confidence Interval (CI)
A range of values calculated from data that expresses statistical uncertainty around an estimated parameter.

### Effect Modification
A situation in which the association between an exposure and outcome differs across levels of another variable.

### Hazard Ratio (HR)
A measure comparing the instantaneous rate of an event between groups over time, commonly used in survival analysis.

### Incidence Rate
The rate at which new cases of a disease or outcome occur in a population over person-time at risk.

### Odds Ratio (OR)
A measure of association comparing the odds of exposure or outcome between groups, commonly used in case-control studies and logistic regression.

### Person-Time
The total amount of time contributed by individuals while they are at risk for an outcome in a study population.

### Population Attributable Fraction (PAF)
The proportion of cases in a population that would be prevented if a specified exposure were eliminated, assuming a causal relationship.

### Prevalence Ratio
A measure comparing the prevalence of a condition or outcome between exposed and unexposed groups.

### Relative Risk / Risk Ratio (RR)
A measure comparing the probability of an outcome in an exposed group with the probability in an unexposed group.

### Standardized Mortality Ratio (SMR)
The ratio of observed deaths in a study population to the number expected based on rates in a reference population.

### Years of Life Lost (YLL)
A measure of premature mortality calculated from the number of deaths and the difference between age at death and a reference life expectancy.


## Risk Assessment and Regulatory Science

### Acute Reference Dose (ARfD)
An estimate of the amount of a substance that can be ingested over a short period, usually one day or less, without appreciable health risk.

### Acceptable Operator Exposure Level (AOEL)
A health-based exposure value used to assess occupational or operator exposure, especially in pesticide regulatory assessments.

### Cancer Slope Factor (CSF)
A parameter used to estimate the increased cancer risk associated with lifetime exposure to a carcinogenic substance.

### Derived No-Effect Level (DNEL)
A regulatory exposure level under European chemical safety frameworks below which adverse effects are not expected.

### Derived Minimal Effect Level (DMEL)
A regulatory exposure level used when a no-effect threshold cannot be established, often for genotoxic carcinogens.

### Health-Based Guidance Value (HBGV)
A general term for exposure values intended to protect human health, including values such as ADI, TDI, RfD, and ARfD.

### Hazard Characterization
The evaluation of the nature, severity, and dose-response characteristics of adverse effects associated with a substance or stressor.

### Hazard Identification
The determination of whether a substance, agent, or condition has the potential to cause adverse effects.

### Margin of Exposure (MOE)
The ratio between a toxicological point of departure and an estimated human exposure level.

### Margin of Safety (MOS)
A ratio comparing a no-effect or low-effect dose with an estimated exposure level, often used to evaluate whether exposure is sufficiently below levels of concern.

### Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL)
A legally enforceable concentration limit for a contaminant in drinking water or another regulated medium.

### Maximum Residue Limit / Tolerance (MRL)
The highest legally permitted concentration of a pesticide, veterinary drug, or contaminant residue in food, feed, or another regulated commodity.

### Minimal Risk Level (MRL)
An estimate developed by the Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry of daily human exposure to a hazardous substance that is unlikely to cause appreciable noncancer health risk over a specified duration.

### Permitted Daily Exposure (PDE)
A health-based limit for daily exposure to a substance, commonly used in pharmaceutical, occupational, and impurity risk assessments.

### Provisional Tolerable Weekly Intake (PTWI)
An estimate of the amount of a contaminant that can be ingested weekly over a lifetime without appreciable health risk, often used for substances with long biological half-lives.

### Reference Value
A health-protective exposure estimate used in risk assessment, such as an RfD, RfC, ADI, TDI, or related regulatory value.

### Risk Characterization
The integration of hazard, dose-response, exposure, and uncertainty information to estimate and describe risk.

### Tolerable Daily Intake (TDI)
An estimate of the amount of a substance that can be ingested daily over a lifetime without appreciable health risk, commonly used for contaminants rather than intentionally added substances.

### Unit Risk
An estimate of increased cancer risk associated with continuous lifetime exposure to a specified unit concentration of a carcinogen.

### Weight of Evidence (WOE)
A structured evaluation of the strength, consistency, quality, and relevance of evidence used to support a scientific or regulatory conclusion.


## Analytical Chemistry and Laboratory Methods

### Blank Correction
Adjustment of analytical results to account for signal or contamination measured in procedural, field, or instrument blanks.

### Detection Limit / Limit of Detection (LOD)
The lowest amount or concentration of an analyte that can be reliably distinguished from background noise under specified analytical conditions.

### Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS)
An analytical technique that separates volatile or semi-volatile compounds by gas chromatography and identifies or quantifies them using mass spectrometry.

### High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC)
An analytical separation technique that uses liquid mobile phases and high-pressure flow through a column to separate chemical components.

### Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS)
An analytical technique used to detect and quantify elements and isotopes at trace or ultra-trace levels.

### Limit of Quantification (LOQ)
The lowest amount or concentration of an analyte that can be quantified with acceptable precision and accuracy under specified conditions.

### Matrix Effect
Alteration of analytical signal caused by other components in the sample matrix, potentially affecting accuracy or sensitivity.

### Method Detection Limit (MDL)
A statistically derived estimate of the minimum concentration of an analyte that can be measured and reported with specified confidence using a particular method.

### Quality Assurance / Quality Control (QA/QC)
A set of planned procedures and performance checks used to ensure that analytical data are reliable, accurate, precise, and fit for purpose.

### Recovery
The proportion of an analyte successfully extracted, processed, and measured from a sample relative to a known or expected amount.

### Solid-Phase Extraction (SPE)
A sample preparation technique that uses a solid sorbent to isolate, concentrate, or clean up analytes from liquids or extracts.

### Tandem Mass Spectrometry (MS/MS)
A mass spectrometry technique involving multiple stages of ion selection and fragmentation to improve identification and quantification of analytes.

### Total Ion Chromatogram (TIC)
A chromatographic trace representing the summed signal of all detected ions over time in a mass spectrometry analysis.
